Aim of the Experiment

Aim of the Virtual Lab Website

This virtual laboratory aims to provide an immersive digital learning environment where students can explore the mathematical concept of orthogonal trajectories through interactive simulations, self-paced assessments, and comprehensive theoretical resources.

The primary goal is to bridge the gap between theoretical differential equations and practical visualization by allowing learners to experiment with various curve families and observe their orthogonal counterparts in real-time.

Furthermore, the platform seeks to enhance conceptual clarity and problem-solving skills in engineering mathematics by offering a structured, step-by-step approach from pre-test evaluation to post-test certification.

Through this integrated web-based tool, students will gain the ability to independently derive orthogonal trajectories for different families of curves and understand their significance in fields like electrostatics, fluid dynamics, and heat transfer.

Ultimately, this website aims to make advanced mathematical learning accessible, engaging, and effective for every student, fostering deeper analytical thinking and practical application skills.

Theory: Orthogonal Trajectory

What are Orthogonal Trajectories?

Orthogonal trajectories are families of curves that intersect another family of curves at right angles (90°). In mathematics, particularly in differential equations, finding orthogonal trajectories is an important application.

Mathematical Definition

Given a one-parameter family of curves F(x, y, c) = 0, an orthogonal trajectory is a curve that intersects each curve of the family at a right angle.

Method to Find Orthogonal Trajectories

  1. Find the differential equation of the given family by eliminating the parameter
  2. Replace dy/dx with -dx/dy (or -1/(dy/dx)) to get the differential equation of orthogonal trajectories
  3. Solve this new differential equation to obtain the family of orthogonal trajectories

Visualizing Orthogonal Trajectories: Straight Lines & Circles

The graph below shows two families of curves that are orthogonal trajectories of each other. Blue curves represent straight lines through the origin: y = m x (family of lines). Red curves represent circles centered at origin: x² + y² = C (orthogonal trajectories). Notice how each red circle intersects every blue line at a 90° angle. The slope of a line (m₁) and the slope of the circle at the intersection point (m₂) satisfy m₁ × m₂ = -1.

Slope Relation at Intersection Point:

For a straight line y = mx, slope m₁ = m.

For a circle x² + y² = C, differentiate: 2x + 2y(dy/dx) = 0 ⇒ dy/dx = -x/y = m₂.

At the intersection point (x₀, y₀) on the circle and line, y₀ = m x₀, so m₂ = -x₀/(m x₀) = -1/m.

Therefore, m₁ × m₂ = m × (-1/m) = -1, confirming the curves are orthogonal (intersect at 90°).

Example: Orthogonal Trajectories of Straight Lines

Problem:

Find the orthogonal trajectories of the family of straight lines through the origin: y = mx

Solution:

1. Given family: y = mx

2. Differentiate: dy/dx = m

3. But from y = mx, m = y/x

4. So differential equation is: dy/dx = y/x

5. For orthogonal trajectories, replace dy/dx with -dx/dy

6. So: -dx/dy = y/x ⇒ x dx + y dy = 0

7. Integrate: x²/2 + y²/2 = c/2 ⇒ x² + y² = c

8. Orthogonal trajectories are circles centered at origin

Applications of Orthogonal Trajectories

  • Electrostatics: Electric field lines and equipotential surfaces are orthogonal
  • Fluid Dynamics: Streamlines and equipotential lines in ideal fluid flow
  • Heat Transfer: Isotherms and heat flow lines
  • Cartography: Lines of longitude and latitude on Earth

Recommended Books and Resources

Here are some excellent references for learning more about orthogonal trajectories and differential equations:

Textbooks:

  • Advanced Engineering Mathematics by Erwin Kreyszig - Chapter 1 covers differential equations and orthogonal trajectories in detail

  • Mathematics for Engineers by Anthony Croft and Robert Davison - Practical approach with engineering applications
